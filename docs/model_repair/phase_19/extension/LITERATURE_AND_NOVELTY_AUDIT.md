# Literature matrix for the engineering extension

Publisher, proceedings or author sources were checked. Where only an abstract was used, the discussion is limited to the stated method and assumptions. The earlier local Phase 19 audit contains the wider verified review. This scoped review tests whether the selected question already has a generic solution.

| Primary or authoritative source | Question, method and assumptions | Evidence, limitation and relation |
|---|---|---|
| Charles F. Manski (2005), Partial identification with missing data: concepts and findings, International Journal of Approximate Reasoning 39, 151–165. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijar.2004.10.006) | Characterizes compatible population laws by allowing alternative missing distributions. | Direct antecedent of the missing-mass hull. Supplies the logic, not project-specific empirical bounds. |
| Peter M. Aronow and Donald K. K. Lee (2013), Interval estimation of population means under unknown but bounded probabilities of sample selection, Biometrika 100, 235–240. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/ass064) | Bounds Hájek estimates with defensible selection-probability limits. | Different inputs. This extension permits completely unreachable units and assumes bounds on their total mass and tail rate. |
| Matthew J. Tudball, Rachael A. Hughes, Kate Tilling, Jack Bowden and Qingyuan Zhao (2023), Sample-constrained partial identification with application to selection bias, Biometrika 110, 485–498. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asac042) | Asymptotic inference with estimable auxiliary constraints. | Simulation and UK Biobank example show use of auxiliary margins. GENEVRA currently stipulates bounds and does not estimate constraints from data. |
| Donald B. Rubin (1976), Inference and missing data, Biometrika 63, 581–592. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/63.3.581) | Conditions under which ignoring missingness in likelihood or Bayesian inference can be justified. | The planted missing tail is deliberately informative. No ignorability claim is available. |
| Sharon L. Lohr and J. N. K. Rao (2006), Estimation in Multiple-Frame Surveys, JASA 101, 1019–1030. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000000195) | Design-based inference combining overlapping frame samples. | Needs defensible frame coverage and overlap data. Two independent seeds supply neither. |
| Reg Baker, J. Michael Brick, Nancy A. Bates, Mike Battaglia, Mick P. Couper, Jill A. Dever, Krista J. Gile and Roger Tourangeau (2013), Summary Report of the AAPOR Task Force on Non-probability Sampling, Journal of Survey Statistics and Methodology 1, 90–143. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jssam/smt008) | Undercoverage and selection require population and inclusion evidence. | An external reference design can inform assumptions. Reproducibility does not establish target representation. |
| Xiao-Li Meng (2018), Statistical paradises and paradoxes in big data (I): Law of large populations, big data paradox, and the 2016 US presidential election, Annals of Applied Statistics 12, 685–726. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/18-AOAS1161SF) | Separates sample size, outcome heterogeneity and selection correlation. | Election illustration demonstrates why larger biased samples need not help. A known missing-mass envelope addresses a different quantified input. |
| Herman Chernoff (1952), A Measure of Asymptotic Efficiency for Tests of a Hypothesis Based on the Sum of Observations, Annals of Mathematical Statistics 23, 493–507. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177729330) | Exponential bounds from moment-generating functions. | Bernoulli specialization yields KL tail inversion. This is an established fixed-look procedure and assumes valid latent counts. |
| Wassily Hoeffding (1963), Probability Inequalities for Sums of Bounded Random Variables, JASA 58, 13–30. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830) | Finite-sample concentration for bounded independent variables. | Supports the primary paired interval and planning proxy. Worst-case bounds ignore rare-event variances and are not guaranteed to yield optimal realized width. |
| Tommy Wright (2014), A Simple Method of Exact Optimal Sample Allocation under Stratification with Any Mixed Constraint Patterns, U.S. Census Bureau research report RRS2014-07. [Report](https://www.census.gov/library/working-papers/2014/adrm/rrs2014-07.html) | Integer stratified allocation for variance minimization under constraints. | Demonstrates that exact allocation is established. Here the two-stratum search minimizes a different conservative confidence-width proxy. No general allocation novelty. |
| Ian Waudby-Smith and Aaditya Ramdas (2024), Estimating means of bounded random variables by betting, JRSS B 86, 1–27. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jrsssb/qkad009) | Variance-adaptive confidence intervals and confidence sequences. | Supports more efficient inference on valid observations. Cannot infer the missing stratum. Optional stopping was not introduced. |
| Ryan J. Tibshirani, Rina Foygel Barber, Emmanuel J. Candès and Aaditya Ramdas (2019), Conformal Prediction Under Covariate Shift, NeurIPS 32. [Proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/8fb21ee7a2207526da55a679f0332de2-Abstract.html) | Predictive coverage under weighted exchangeability and density ratios. | Assumption-conditional prediction differs from confidence coverage of a population mean. Invisible strata cannot be reweighted from observed overlap alone. |
| Yachong Yang, Arun Kumar Kuchibhotla and Eric Tchetgen Tchetgen (2024), Doubly robust calibration of prediction sets under covariate shift, JRSS B 86, 943–965. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jrsssb/qkae009) | Doubly robust predictive calibration using target covariates and invariant conditional outcome law. | Does not handle both channels missing relevant outcome mass without additional information. |
| Yonatan Geifman and Ran El-Yaniv (2019), SelectiveNet: A Deep Neural Network with an Integrated Reject Option, ICML, PMLR 97, 2151–2159. [Proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/geifman19a.html) | Optimizes a selective risk and coverage objective. | Supports reporting useful decision coverage and refusal. The anchor certificate here is a different object. |
| Anastasios N. Angelopoulos, Stephen Bates, Emmanuel J. Candès, Michael I. Jordan and Lihua Lei (2025), Learn then test: Calibrating predictive algorithms to achieve risk control, Annals of Applied Statistics 19, 1641–1662. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/24-AOAS1998) | Held-out calibration and multiplicity control for prespecified losses. | Gives risk control under the relevant calibration law, not a guarantee of transport. Used as a future empirical benchmark only if independent outcomes exist. |
| U.S. Census Bureau, 2020 Post-Enumeration Survey, official technical release index. [Documentation](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/decennial-census/about/coverage-measurement/pes.2020.html) | Separate probability survey and linkage inform coverage-error measurement. | Practical example of independently governed evidence. It is not evidence for GENEVRA and requires assumptions about linkage and coverage. |

Primary studies in this matrix support the relevant methods, not priority for this particular benchmark. Shared-blindness detection without external information is unidentifiable. An implementable research gap remains in how verified external restrictions, acquisition cost and rare-event precision interact for this specific decision.
