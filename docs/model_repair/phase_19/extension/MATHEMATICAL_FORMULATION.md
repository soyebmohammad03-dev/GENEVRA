# Mathematical formulation

Let r be the tail probability conditional on reachability, m the inaccessible target mass and z the tail probability in that mass. The target tail probability is p=(1−m)r+mz. The reachable weights are known conditional weights, not target inclusion probabilities. For simultaneous confidence band R on r and defensible sets M and Z, the target confidence region is the image of R×M×Z. This map is affine in each coordinate holding the others fixed. The eight vertices attain its extrema. Continuity gives every intermediate value. The region is sharp for the rectangular input information and no further dependence restrictions.

For independent Bernoulli counts k_h of n_h, inversion retains candidates satisfying n_h KL(k_h/n_h || r_h) ≤ log(4/alpha). Each directional Chernoff bound spends alpha/4 and union across two strata gives at least 1−alpha simultaneous coverage. Mix endpoints with the known reachable weights. On that event, if M and Z contain the true inaccessible quantities, the envelope covers p. External bounds here are fixed assumptions. If they are estimated, add their miscoverage to the error budget or use a joint construction. The current implementation does not derive external bounds or verify their substantive validity.

The mean is c+(t−c)p with externally fixed c=.30 and t=234.50. Let a=.004 be the supplied anchor tail mass. An anchor certificate requires max(|pL−a|,|pU−a|)(t−c)≤.30. A positive threshold call needs mean lower bound >.60. A nonbeneficial call needs upper bound ≤.60. They answer different questions. Conditional coverage after issuance is not guaranteed by the marginal confidence statement.

Pinsker's inequality bounds each KL interval radius by sqrt(log(4/alpha)/(2n_h)). With m capped at U and z in [0,1], the score interval width is at most (t−c) times the sum of the reachable width and U. A proxy for the controllable part is sum(w_h/sqrt(n_h)). Positive tariffs c_h constrain sum(c_h n_h)≤B. Its continuous optimum has n_h proportional to (w_h/c_h)^(2/3), by the Lagrange equations. The implemented two-stratum integer optimum enumerates n_1, chooses the largest feasible n_0, and keeps the best proxy. This is O(B/c_1) time and O(1) extra space. It minimizes this proxy, not the realized KL width or uncertainty due to missingness.

Even if r is known exactly, with m∈[0,U] and z∈[0,1] the interval for p has width U. If U=.005, the score width floor is 1.171, above the .60 target. More reachable samples cannot remove that floor. If the shared-blind law can instead have missing mass .004 with z=1, claiming U=.0005 can exclude the true target even with perfect knowledge of r. Tight unverified z bounds cause the same kind of failure.
