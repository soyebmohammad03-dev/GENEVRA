# Literature and novelty audit

This targeted audit follows the [Phase 20 methodology](../METHODOLOGY.md) and [Phase 21 literature audit](../phase_21/LITERATURE_AND_NOVELTY.md). It uses author, publisher and agency sources, with no exhaustiveness claim. Publisher abstracts or repository summaries support only the high-level comparisons below. Design details for the Census and EPA materials come from their agency records.

| Primary source | Established contribution and overlap | Remaining GENEVRA distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Guido W. Imbens and Charles F. Manski (2004), *Confidence Intervals for Partially Identified Parameters*, Econometrica 72(6), 1845–1857. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2004.00555.x) | Distinguishes identification bounds and confidence intervals in partially identified models. Sampling and identification uncertainty already have a formal treatment. | Executable known-truth case shows an identification floor and its consequences for three separate decision endpoints. |
| Charles F. Manski (2005), *Partial Identification with Missing Data: Concepts and Findings*, International Journal of Approximate Reasoning 39(2–3), 151–165. | Matthew J. Tudball, Rachael A. Credibility of the restrictions matters. | Varies the validity of supplied omission evidence and reports decision outcomes when it fails. |

Partial information about selection can be sufficient for useful bounds. | Here an omitted-mass bound is a distinct externally supplied constraint, and benchmark outcomes include abstention and certificate validity. |
| Xiao-Li Meng (2018), *Statistical paradises and paradoxes in big data (I): Law of large populations, big data paradox, and the 2016 US presidential election*, Annals of Applied Statistics 12(2), 685–726. This is a close precedent for the proposed area audit. | GENEVRA's proposed frame audit is an application of an established design idea. | Tommy Wright (2019), *Direct Proof of Exact Sample Allocation Optimality with Cost Constraints*, US Census Bureau Research Report RRS2019-03. [Report](https://www.census.gov/library/working-papers/2019/adrm/RRS2019-03.html) | Exact stratified allocation under a cost constraint for variance. | GENEVRA optimizes a stipulated Hoeffding width proxy. Its observed failure against an equal-spend comparator shows that proxy optimality and actual precision can diverge. |
| US EPA (2024), *National Rivers and Streams Assessment 2018–19: Technical Support Document*. [Agency report](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-12/nrsa-2018-19-tsd-final-11252024.pdf) | Field reference for probability sampling, design weighting and QA. Its stream-mile target differs from the proposed reach-week target. | Informs protocol feasibility. Supplies no GENEVRA policy outcomes. |

The narrow originality claim is a particular reproducible benchmark and its linked results under stated information and cost assumptions. The reviewed literature already establishes missing-data bounds, frame auditing, bounded confidence procedures, abstention and cost-aware allocation. No search result establishes benchmark uniqueness. An external reviewer should locate the closest benchmark, map overlapping regimes, and assess whether pairing the failure modes with exact replay makes the artifact scientifically useful.
