# Publication strategy and decision gates

| Route | Contribution and framing | Evidence required before submission | Main rejection risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Negative methodological feasibility | Under specified shared-omission regimes, calibration failed to deliver safe and useful certification. Frame as a scoped negative result. | Credible comparator set, stronger connection to operational regimes, and independent statistical review of the exact claim and its practical importance | Known identification result with project-specific synthetic illustrations |
| B. Reproducible benchmark and systems | Archived known-truth failure cases, matched-access policy comparisons, and replay checkers expose distinct failures in coverage, certification and useful precision | Independent replay, strongest relevant baseline and access audit, explicit comparison to closest benchmark, evidence an independent user can apply the suite | Polished archive without demonstrated research reuse or distinction from prior suites |
| C. Prospective empirical validation | Matched-cost policy evaluation with independently governed frame and blinded references | Qualified partner, approved pilot and data governance, successful operational gates, powered cluster/design-valid registration, held-out empirical outcomes and external review | Unidentified reference target, inadequate independence or power, and uncertain costs |

**Primary route B.** Send the package for external technical scrutiny. Hold journal submission until the benchmark evidence meets the gates. **Fallback route A as a scoped technical report** if a benchmark-paper contribution cannot be distinguished. An A journal submission also requires independent adjudication of scientific importance and comparator fairness. Route C remains blocked.

| Gate | Decision | Scope and evidence that would change the decision |
|---|---|---|
| Mathematical correctness | PASS | Fixed-law width calculation and checked inversion under declared assumptions. Reopen with a counterexample or changed target law. No theorem claim beyond assumptions. |
| Statistical validity | CONDITIONAL | Frozen comparisons use declared units and laws. A broader claim needs appropriate sampling and misclassification bounds, credible omission evidence, predeclared multiplicity and dependence handling. |
| Originality | CONDITIONAL | A particular replayable stress suite is distinguishable from its established components. Need direct comparison to closest benchmark and independent assessment of substantive difference. |
| Reproducibility | PASS | Public archived reconstruction and clean internal replay. External replay remains a separate gate. |
| Evidence sufficiency | FAIL | Current evidence supports the bounded internal benchmark. To submit, independently verify relevance and fairness, strengthen baseline coverage, and demonstrate reuse or practical significance for the chosen scope. |
| External review | FAIL | Three internal simulations only. Require substantive review and a reported independent reconstruction with conflict and independence disclosures. |
| Empirical validation | FAIL | Census description and planning fixtures only. Require independent outcome acquisition and a powered matched policy comparison. |
| Ethics and data governance | FAIL for fieldwork | Require documented rights, independent custodian, permissions and ethics determination, safety approval, data-use terms and release plan. |

**Decision.** Ready to invite external scrutiny. Journal submission and field recruitment remain on hold. The next evidence is independent artifact reconstruction and adversarial feedback, followed by scope and comparator revisions. Partner qualification remains a separate prospective workstream.
