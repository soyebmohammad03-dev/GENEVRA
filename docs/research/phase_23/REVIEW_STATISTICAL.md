# Statistical methodology review

**Recommendation.** Hold the negative methods submission. Consider a benchmark submission after substantial statistical revision and external adjudication.

## Major weaknesses and required revisions

1. **Identification precedes inference.** The fixed-support omission calculation is correct at exact reachable rate and a valid mass cap. It says nothing about where a real cap comes from. Add a counterexample with common coverage error in the custodian and channels, a worked ratio bound from a probability area design, and a full-support fallback for an unidentified audit denominator. Separate confidence in the cap from coverage conditional on its validity.
2. **Risk definitions and denominators differ.** Phase 18 false-certification counts use synthetic runs. Conditional risk needs issued decisions as denominator, and can be undefined. Phase 22's adverse proxy includes unresolved issued certificates on the joint reference event. Require separate issuance, all-run adverse risk, conditional risk and useful gain with uncertainty. A reference family allocation does not confer arbitrary conditional-on-issuance coverage.
3. **Field uncertainty remains unimplemented.** Binomial safety and paired Hoeffding require an independent episode design with a declared target sampling law. Shared watersheds, crews or seasons can break that argument. Misclassification of apparently resolved reference outcomes is distinct from missing labels. Require a design-valid reference estimator with simultaneous omission and label-error bounds, design sensitivity and a predeclared failure path.
4. **Novelty is narrow.** Existing identification and calibration methods are sufficient to explain the algebra. Require a direct comparison to the closest benchmark before advancing an inferential novelty claim.

## Minor weaknesses

Use different symbols for threshold, tolerance and precision. Give both numerator and denominator in each risk table. Show the width-floor figure with axes labeled by valid cap and score units. Preserve the phase-specific distinction between descriptive and simultaneous uncertainty.
