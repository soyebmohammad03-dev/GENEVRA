# Comparator literature and implementation

| Neyman (1934), *On the Two Different Aspects of the Representative Method*. [JRSS](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2397-2335.1934.tb04184.x) | Minimize the variance of a stratified mean. Under equal cost the continuous allocation is proportional to stratum weight times SD. | Known weights and known or estimated SD. Equal sample size constraint. | Generalize to unequal tariffs. A variance optimum need not optimize coverage with a width threshold. |
| Wright (2019), *Direct Proof of Exact Sample Allocation Optimality with Cost Constraints*. | Statistics Canada, *Survey Methods and Practices*, catalog 12-587-X. [Agency text](https://www.statcan.gc.ca/n1/en/pub/12-587-x/12-587-x2003001-eng.pdf) | Proportional independent units use stratum weights. Neither requires variance estimates. | Both suit this benchmark. Equal spending buys unequal independent units when prices differ. | Bernoulli bound \(p(1-p)\le 1/4\), applied to Wright's variance objective. | Minimize worst-case sampling variance over independently unrestricted stratum rates. | Weights and costs. | Horvitz and Thompson (1952), *A Generalization of Sampling Without Replacement From a Finite Universe*. ## Derivation and information

For independent Bernoulli inference samples with weights \(w_h\), variance is \(\sum w_h^2 p_h(1-p_h)/n_h\) in tail-rate units. Multiplying by the fixed score span squared does not change the optimum. The Lagrange condition under \(\sum c_h n_h=B\) gives \(n_h\propto w_h\sqrt{p_h(1-p_h)/c_h}\) in the interior. The runner enumerates all feasible second-stratum counts and admits a first-stratum count only when it exactly exhausts the budget and clears the minimum. Ties use the first minimum in increasing second-stratum count. Missing, invalid or infeasible information raises an error. The minimax fixed arm is the separately labeled no-variance fallback.

The operational policy estimates \(p_h\) as \((k_h+.5)/(128+1)\) from the paid pilot. Smoothing avoids zero variance at all-zero or all-one pilots and may substantially overstate very rare rates. The oracle uses latent rates and is separated in every table. The original GENEVRA proxy minimizes \(\sum w_h/\sqrt{n_h}\) on the remaining budget. All inference uses the same conditional fixed-look bounds because allocation precedes an independent inference stage. Count pairing retains the same prefix draws across policies. Every matched method is charged the same pilot and inference budget, including fixed methods which could skip the pilot in a separate deployment design.
