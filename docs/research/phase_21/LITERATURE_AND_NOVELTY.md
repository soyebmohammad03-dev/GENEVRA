# Contribution and novelty assessment

**Central question.** At equal information access and charged acquisition cost, can an omission-aware certificate increase useful decisions while controlling false certification on an independently enumerated target, and what external information is needed for that claim to be testable?

The project currently solves a narrower task. It makes declared sampling, support and cost assumptions executable, and exposes their failures in frozen known-law benchmarks. The source-anchor error, population-mean coverage and threshold call are distinct outcomes. The project has not demonstrated a generally superior policy. Its conditional envelope uses established partial identification and concentration, and its allocation proxy failed the frozen comparison.

| Closest primary work | Question, estimand and design | Assumptions and limitation | Difference and evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aronow and Lee (2013), *Interval estimation of population means under unknown but bounded probabilities of sample selection*, Biometrika 100, 235–240. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/ass064) | Bound a finite-population mean under bounded selection probabilities, using sharp Hájek-estimator bounds. | Probability limits are substantive. Uniform positive reach is a different restriction from completely inaccessible mass. | Closest mean-estimation comparator if bounds on inclusion can be independently justified. GENEVRA uses explicit omitted mass and tail-support restrictions. This difference is a formulation choice, without a new theorem. |
| Tudball, Hughes, Tilling, Bowden and Zhao (2023), *Sample-constrained partial identification with application to selection bias*, Biometrika 110, 485–498. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asac042) | Inference with estimated auxiliary constraints, simulations and a selected UK Biobank example. | Relies on a defended constraint set and asymptotic conditions. Cannot establish arbitrary missing outcomes. | More general treatment of estimated constraints. GENEVRA assumes supplied bounds and isolates budget and refusal under known laws. Real estimation of bounds requires a separate uncertainty budget. |
| Manski (2005), *Partial identification with missing data: concepts and findings*, IJAR 39, 151–165. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijar.2004.10.006) | Identify sets by combining observed and admissible missing-data laws. | Width remains even with infinite observed data when missing outcome laws are unconstrained. | Direct antecedent of the coverage envelope and identification floor. Shared blindness is an application of established non-identification. |
| Lohr and Rao (2006), *Estimation in Multiple-Frame Surveys*, JASA 101, 1019–1030. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000000195) | Combine probability samples from multiple frames for a finite-population estimand. | Needs defended union coverage, inclusion design and overlap information. | Two acquisition streams are not a valid multi-frame estimator absent those inputs. Use as a comparator only if complete design metadata exists. |
| Tibshirani, Barber, Candès and Ramdas (2019), *Conformal Prediction Under Covariate Shift*, NeurIPS 32. [Proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/8fb21ee7a2207526da55a679f0332de2-Abstract.html) | Prediction-set coverage with changed covariate weights. | Weighted exchangeability or a defensible density ratio and overlap. Prediction coverage differs from confidence coverage for a target mean. | Ineligible as a primary mean/anchor baseline. A separate prediction benchmark would require its own estimand and constraints. |
| Angelopoulos, Bates, Candès, Jordan and Lei (2025), *Learn then test: Calibrating predictive algorithms to achieve risk control*, AOAS 19, 1641–1662. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/24-AOAS1998) | Test candidate policies on independent calibration data to control declared risks with multiplicity. | Requires the right calibration law, losses and independent evaluation. Cannot correct shared undercoverage. | Strong comparator for held-out risk control when both methods receive equivalent calibration episodes. GENEVRA's anchor error is a different decision loss. |
| U.S. Census Bureau PES and DA, 2022, and GAO (2024), *2020 Census: Coverage Errors and Challenges Inform 2030 Plans*. [GAO-25-107160](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-25-107160/index.html) | Separate collection and administrative accounting to evaluate population counts. | Nonidentical coverage universes and different linkage/administrative assumptions. | External precedent for separate target access. The Phase 21 diagnostic reconstructs selected published quantities. It provides no evidence of GENEVRA's operating characteristics. |

The literature was screened using publisher records, author manuscripts and agency technical documents. The closest cases are partial identification with selection constraints, multiple-frame sampling and risk calibration. This targeted comparison does not establish priority for a benchmark design.

## Novelty matrix and positioning

| Candidate claim | Status | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Shared missingness creates an irreducible identification region | Established theory | Cite prior work. No novelty claim. |
| Matched-access benchmark exhibits false certificates despite correct threshold direction | Distinct project-specific stress result | An independently specified external setting would establish relevance beyond the planted case. |
| Conservative integer allocation can harm realized rare-event precision | Observed for the frozen proxy, laws and budgets | Compare to variance-aware and design-based allocation with real costs, separate pilots and held-out evaluation. |
| Valid omitted-mass bounds restore coverage | Conditional synthetic result and mathematical propagation | Independent bounds and explicit uncertainty allocation in a real target. |
| Better statistical method, field savings or validated biological assay | Unsupported | Comparative evidence and independent references under appropriate endpoints. |

**Best framing is C, a reproducible benchmark and evaluation framework with negative methodological feasibility findings (B).** A methodology paper (A) lacks a new general result. An applied paper (D) needs linked external outcomes and measured cost. Journal submission remains on hold. The narrow claim is an audit-ready benchmark that separates equal information, omission uncertainty, and the value of certification at declared budgets. It does not generalize the observed failure rate to the field.
