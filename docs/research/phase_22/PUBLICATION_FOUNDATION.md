# Publication foundation update

**Working title** — When calibration shares a blind spot: matched-access stress tests and a design for independent validation.

**Abstract.** We separate calibration precision, identification under shared undercoverage, and the utility of certificate decisions. Frozen synthetic comparisons expose shared-blind failures and a loss of realized precision under a conservative costed allocation proxy. A public Census diagnostic reproduces selected aggregate coverage figures and illustrates why reference construction matters. We specify a prospective field comparison with separately governed target enumeration, blinded adjudication and costed access. This design has not been executed. External partner access, reference precision and empirical performance remain unknown.

| Evidence class | Supportable claim | Needed before stronger claims |
|---|---|---|
| Completed synthetic studies | Phase 18 shared blindness produced 180 false certificates in 512 runs with zero mean coverage. Phase 19 valid supplied bounds restored coverage in one planted case, and the allocation proxy reduced covered precision. | External setting with measured cost, defended omission evidence and independent outcomes. |
| Reconstructed external diagnostic | Phase 21 reconstructed −5.46% national Census net error for ages 0–4 from published count and estimate. | It cannot identify the jointly omitted mass or GENEVRA's certificate risk. |
| Proposed prospective validation | Phase 22 defines independent frame custody, ascertainment audit, finite-population reference, matched-cost scoring and gates. | A partner, roster, separate audit, permissions, QC and adequate independent episodes. No field result exists. |
| Established theory | Exact intervals, missing-data bounds and finite-population inference explain the mechanism. | No new general statistical estimator or identification theorem is claimed. |

The strongest route remains a reproducible benchmark with negative feasibility results. First obtain an independent artifact assessment. Next establish access and reference feasibility with one independently governed partner. If the small pilot passes, preregister a powered design only if costs and scientific value justify it. Do not pursue superiority or biological claims on the basis of a completed protocol. Journal submission remains on hold.
