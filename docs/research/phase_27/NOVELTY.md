# GENEVRA closest-work audit

This focused review searched publisher, agency, author and software records for combinations of shared frame omissions, survey nonresponse, MNAR evaluation, bounded means, risk certification, cost-sensitive allocation and simulation benchmarks. It compared published methods, data access and targets. It did not reexecute third-party software or establish exhaustive priority.

## Critical novelty decision

- **A, benchmark alone.** Useful because shared frame blindness is distinct from item deletion. However, a synthetic example of a standard identification problem may be too narrow by itself.
- **B, diagnostic framework alone.** Useful for denominators and attribution. Interval coverage, precision, refusal and error risk already have established definitions.
- **C, combined benchmark and verification.** Strongest route. The specific combination of shared blind-spot stress cases, information and tariff controls, negative allocation results and separately implemented replication is the contribution under review. No new estimator, general guarantee or priority claim is supported.
- **D, negative methodological feasibility.** Credible fallback as a scoped technical report. A journal case would need evidence that the negative finding changes a real methodological decision beyond the planted law.

Publication would be unsuitable if external readers cannot reproduce the artifact, if the closest framework already provides equivalent cases and endpoints without material added value, or if a claim depends on unseen target outcomes or implausible field costs. The empirical claims require independent frames and outcomes. The benchmark claims require documented external reproduction, independent comparison to the nearest artifact and a separately governed holdout before claiming transfer.
