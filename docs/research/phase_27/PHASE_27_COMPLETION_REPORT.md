It uses a discrete convex search and a closed-form omission hull. In the primary cell the width proxy produced 1/256 useful intervals, paid-pilot Neyman 104/256 and equal spend 229/256. All 103 discordant pairs favored Neyman. The paired difference was −40.23 percentage points with 95% interval [−57.21, −23.26]. All 17 Bonferroni-adjusted secondary intervals included zero.

From Phase 18 frozen endpoints, certification flags and raw anchor counts, the reference checked 40 method cells, three adjusted utility contrasts and nine simultaneous exact safety bounds. The shared-blind case retained 0/512 coverage, 180/512 false certificates and zero wrong threshold calls. Its simultaneous upper adverse-risk bound was .4073. The archived independent checker verifies upstream interval arrays during replay. The Phase 27 scorer does not independently regenerate those Phase 18 endpoints.

## Benchmark and literature

The comparator set supports the fixed synthetic question. True-rate allocation is privileged. No-acquisition refusal and direct reachable calibration have distinct roles. Broader efficiency claims require a registered interval-method comparison, realistic costs and withheld scenarios. The existing stress cases expose jointly omitted and asymmetric frames, invalid weights and caps, rare tails, nonresponse combined with loss of support, and coverage-width trade-offs. They cannot identify deployment prevalence or estimate empirical safety.

Closest inferential software is selectioninterval. Multiple-frame theory and the Norwegian fisheries audit provide direct conceptual and field precedents. The Jäger et al. OpenML imputation benchmark is an adjacent evaluation artifact with a different target. GENEVRA contributes the particular benchmark plus independently implemented verification of its acquisition and decision outputs. It supplies no new statistical estimator or externally confirmed usefulness.
