# Software positioning

This comparison concerns documented interfaces and assumptions. The Phase 29 benchmark compares frozen allocation rules under the registered synthetic design. It supplies no head-to-head software timings or general claims about comparative performance.

| Approach | Objective and assumptions | Methods and coverage diagnostics | Certification and auditability |
|---|---|---|---|
| GENEVRA publication artifact | Stress-test population-mean claims when multiple frames share omissions. Synthetic truth, fixed strata, known data-generation and cost rules, and explicit bounds on omitted mass and values. | Stratified Bernoulli interval aggregation, bounded undercoverage envelope, allocation comparisons, and separate population coverage and useful-precision metrics. | Anchor certificates with abstention and distinct false-certificate denominators. Public frozen counts, manifests, independent numerical implementation and end-to-end replay. |
| `selectioninterval` | Estimate target-population relationships with biased sample selection, under the paper's bounds on inclusion probabilities and estimator assumptions. | Bounds on inverse-probability weights and sensitivity regions for regression estimators. Its examples include OLS and instrumental-variable calculations. | Reports estimator sensitivity intervals. The published software focuses on selection sensitivity rather than the GENEVRA shared-frame certification benchmark. [Author software](https://github.com/matt-tudball/selectioninterval) |
| R `survey` | Estimate population quantities under complex survey designs using supplied design information such as inclusion weights, strata or clusters. | Design-based summaries and regression, variance estimation, and calibration. Diagnosing a shared omission requires additional information about the unsampled units. | Design-based estimates, uncertainty and standard diagnostics. The package exposes documented design objects and reproducible examples. [CRAN reference](https://cran.r-project.org/package=survey) |
| Learn then Test | Control a specified predictive risk using independent calibration data under the method's sampling and testing assumptions. | Finite-sample risk tests and multiple-testing control for selecting predictors. Application to a partly unidentified population mean requires separate identification assumptions. | Accepts predictors meeting the tested risk criterion. The published implementation and examples expose the calibration procedure. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1214/24-AOAS1998) · [Author code](https://github.com/aangelopoulos/ltt) |

GENEVRA combines controlled common omissions, distinct estimands for coverage and certification, matched accounting, raw synthetic evidence, and a separate calculation path in a single reproducible artifact. Its intervals, survey weighting and calibration concepts use established statistical ideas. The registered width-proxy comparison produced a negative result. Whether the integration serves a practical workflow or adds enough value for JSS remains open. An independent researcher would need to exercise a concrete comparison or use case before those claims could be made.
